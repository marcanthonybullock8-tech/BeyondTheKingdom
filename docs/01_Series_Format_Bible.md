## 2. Episodes Per Year — 250

**Answer: 250 new episodes per year.**

- A real daytime soap airs every weekday — about 260 airdates per year.
- In 2005 there are 255 weekday airdates after the usual holiday preemptions (Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, and the Christmas observance).
- Taking out about 5 more days for real-world preemptions (breaking news, sports, presidential addresses) leaves **250**. That is the real network daytime standard for a one-hour soap.
- Every broadcast week is 5 episodes, and the story moves on each weekday.

## 3. Season Numbering — The Broadcast-Season Model

**Answer: Number seasons by the U.S. broadcast TV season, which runs from mid-September to mid-September. Number episodes cumulatively and never reset them.**

Real daytime soaps work this way. They don't restart at "Episode 1" each season. The episode counter runs continuously (for example, *Episode #5,000*), and "seasons" are industry bookkeeping tied to the fall broadcast season. A soap that premieres mid-season (like ours, in January) has a shortened first season.

| Season | Broadcast Window | Airdate Slots | New Episodes | Episode Numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Season 1 (Premiere Season) | Mon Jan 3, 2005 – Fri Sep 16, 2005 | 182 | 180 | #0001 – #0180 |
| Season 2 | Mon Sep 19, 2005 – Fri Sep 15, 2006 | 254 | 250 | #0181 – #0430 |
| Season 3 and beyond | Mid-September to mid-September | ~255 | 250 | Continues cumulatively |

**How to reference an episode:** *Season 1 · Episode #0001 · Monday, January 3, 2005*

**Holidays with no new episode in Season 1:** Memorial Day (Mon May 30, 2005), Independence Day (Mon Jul 4, 2005), Labor Day (Mon Sep 5, 2005). Two more Season 1 dates are held for real-world preemptions.

**Sweeps periods (ratings months — the biggest cliffhangers, weddings, deaths, and reveals land here):**
- February 2005 sweeps: about Feb 3 – Mar 2, 2005
- May 2005 sweeps: about Apr 28 – May 25, 2005
- November 2005 sweeps: about Oct 27 – Nov 23, 2005
- **Season finale:** Friday, September 16, 2005 — the biggest Friday cliffhanger of the year

## 4. Serialized vs. Standalone — 95% / 5%

**Answer: 95% serialized, 5% standalone.**

- **95% serialized:** Every episode continues the storylines from the day before. Storylines overlap, pick up where they left off, and pay off weeks or months later. This is the real daytime formula.
- **5% "standalone-feel" specials:** About 12–13 episodes a year. They still count in continuity, but each one centers on a single event or format:
  - Holiday episodes (Thanksgiving, Christmas, New Year's Eve)
  - Flashback / "family history" episodes about the Donohue legacy
  - A wedding, funeral, or gala shown across one full hour
  - "Two-hander" episodes (a single long confrontation between two characters)
  - Anniversary episodes
- **Continuity rule:** Nothing is ever "reset." Every event, injury, secret, marriage, birth, death, and lie is permanent canon unless the story itself changes it (a reveal, a lie exposed, a return).

### Continuity Protocol
1. **Master Timeline Log:** Every scene carries a date and time stamp.
2. **Age Tracker:** Ages are calculated from locked birthdays and change on each birthday. A character's age on any date is always exact.
3. **No SORAS without an on-screen reason.** ("Soap Opera Rapid Aging" is a common soap trick. We avoid it to keep it real.)
4. **Story Ledger:** An ongoing list of open secrets (who knows what, and since when).
5. **Relationship Map:** Marriages, divorces, affairs, and paternity, all dated.

## 6. Episode Titles

**Answer: Real daytime soaps don't give episodes titles.** Each episode is identified by its **episode number + air date**, plus an internal **production number**.

**Official format:**
> **BEYOND THE KINGDOM — Episode #0001**
> **Air Date: Monday, January 3, 2005**
> **Prod. #BTK-0001**

**Special exception:** Landmark episodes get an in-world promo "event name" for press, ads, and fans. Examples:
- "The Premiere" (Ep #0001)
- "The Donohue Gala" (a sweeps event)
- "The 100th Episode" (Ep #0100)
- "Season Finale" (Ep #0180)

These names appear only in marketing, soap-magazine headlines, and fan chatter. They never replace the number and date.

## 7. Script Length, Acts & Scenes

| Element | Standard |
|---|---|
| Script length | **75–90 pages (target: 80 pages)** for a one-hour daytime episode. Daytime scripts are dialogue-heavy and multi-camera, so they run longer than a primetime hour. |
| Structure | **Teaser (Cold Open) + 6 Acts** |
| Scenes | **24–30 scenes per episode (target: 28)** |
| Storylines per episode | 3–5 storylines rotated through the acts |
| Scenes per act | Teaser: 1–2 · Acts 1–6: 4–5 each |
| Act breaks | Every act ends on a "hook" (a reveal, a slap, a stare, a doorbell). Act 6 ends on the day's cliffhanger. |
| Friday rule | The **Friday cliffhanger** is the biggest of the week. It keeps the audience coming back Monday. |

### Episode Template
1. **Title Card:** Episode #, air date, production #, in-universe dates covered
2. **TEASER (Cold Open):** Picks up exactly where the last episode ended
3. **Main Title Sequence**
4. **ACT ONE – ACT SIX:** Scenes rotate between storylines
5. **Cliffhanger Tag**
6. **"Next on Beyond the Kingdom" preview**
7. **Media & Fan Layer:** News headlines, press, and fan posts reacting to that episode's events

### Screenplay Scene Format
```
INT. [LOCATION] - [DAY/NIGHT]
[Day, Month Date, 2005 — Time]

[Action lines in present tense.]

                    CHARACTER NAME (age)
          (parenthetical)
     Dialogue.
```

## 8. Storytelling Layers (2005 Period Accuracy)

The story is told through three layers:

**A. Screenplay Scenes** — character-driven dialogue that drives the drama.

**B. News & Media (2005 outlets and formats)**
- Local Atlanta print and TV news, business pages, society columns, and gossip columns
- National Black media (magazines, cable entertainment news, radio)
- Business press for Donohue corporate storylines
- Soap magazine coverage (weekly soap digests, covers, spoilers)

**C. The Internet & Fans — only what existed in 2005**
- **Available in 2005:** Soap message boards and forums, AOL, email chains, fan sites, LiveJournal, Xanga, BlackPlanet, MySpace, Blogger/blogs, text messaging (SMS), two-way pagers/Sidekicks/BlackBerrys, flip phones (e.g., Motorola RAZR)
- **Facebook:** Limited to college students in 2005
- **YouTube:** Launches in 2005 (public beta in spring). Not usable in January 2005.
- **Not yet available — never use in 2005 scenes:** Twitter (2006), iPhone (2007), Instagram (2010), smartphone apps, hashtags as a trend culture, streaming services

## 9. Timeline & Age Rules

- Every scene carries an **in-universe date and time** (e.g., *Monday, January 3, 2005 — 7:42 PM EST*).
- Atlanta is in **Eastern Time**. EST applies until DST begins on **Sunday, April 3, 2005**, then EDT until **Sunday, October 30, 2005**.
- **Ages are calculated from each character's exact birthday.** A character born June 10, 1950 is **54** on January 3, 2005 and turns **55** on June 10, 2005.
- Birthdays are on-screen events when they matter to the story.
- One airdate usually covers one story day. Timelines can compress (several episodes covering one gala night) or skip (a time jump announced on screen).
