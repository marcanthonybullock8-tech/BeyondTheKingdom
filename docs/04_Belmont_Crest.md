### The Gates
- **The Main Gate (the Crown Gate):** One Belmont Crest Parkway NW, off West Paces Ferry Road. Twin limestone guardhouses, 24-foot wrought-iron gates with a gilded "BC" crown crest, and a scorch mark on the left pillar that has been deliberately preserved since 1959 (see History).
- **The Service Gate:** Off Paces Ferry Road NW. Staff, deliveries, and contractors.
- **The River Gate:** A private dock and boathouse on the Chattahoochee. Officially closed after dark. **SECRET:** The birthplace of the Blackwater Syndicate (see Document 06).

## THE HISTORY OF BELMONT CREST

### 1955–1957: The Buckhead Gambit
- In 1955 the U.S. Supreme Court rules in *Holmes v. City of Atlanta* that Atlanta's public golf courses must desegregate. On the first integrated day, a white man spits on Alexander's shoes at the North Fulton course. Alexander goes home and tells Simone: *"I'm done asking to play on their land. We'll buy our own."*
- Black families in 1950s Atlanta are pushed to the south and west side. The real estate industry quietly makes sure no white Buckhead seller will sell to a Black buyer.
- **Simone's plan:**
  - Alexander hires a young white attorney, **Harlan Whitcomb**, who hates the Klan more than he likes money.
  - Through Whitcomb and **eleven shell companies** (named after Simone's favorite flowers: Magnolia Land Co., Camellia Holdings, Jasmine Trust, and so on), the Donohues quietly buy **1,140 acres** of Chattahoochee bluff forest from 23 owners over 26 months.
  - The sellers think the buyer is a "Northern timber syndicate."

### 1958: The Founding
- **Saturday, March 15, 1958:** The deeds are consolidated. The **Belmont Crest Association, Inc.** is chartered.
  - The name comes from **Belmont**, Simone's family's plantation-era origin story (her great-grandmother was enslaved on a Louisiana estate called Belmont). **Simone insisted:** *"We will take the name they owned us under and make it mean something they can never touch."*
  - **"Crest"** is for the bluff, and for the family crown.
- **Monday, May 5, 1958:** At a press conference at the Hotel Donohue, **Alexander reveals that the owner of the largest private landholding in Buckhead is a Black man.**
  - The *Atlanta Daily World* runs the headline **"DONOHUE BUYS BUCKHEAD."**
  - White Buckhead panics.
- **Summer 1958:**
  - Groundbreaking on the **Dowager House** (then simply "Simone's House") and the **main gate**.
  - Neighbors petition the city to rezone the land as "unsuitable for residential use."
  - Harlan Whitcomb wins in court.

### 1959: The Fire at the Gate
- **Saturday, June 20, 1959:** **Victor Donohue marries Joan Mercer**, the first wedding at Belmont Crest Chapel. 600 guests attend.
- **Saturday, October 24, 1959, 1:15 AM:** Klansmen burn a 12-foot cross at the Crown Gate.
  - **Simone, 48, stands on the Dowager House porch in her nightgown with a shotgun until dawn.**
  - Theodore (25) and Victor (23) guard the gate with rifles beside her.
  - At 7:00 AM, Simone serves the reporters coffee and biscuits and says: ***"Tell them we're not going anywhere. Tell them we're just getting started."***
  - The scorched gate pillar has been preserved ever since. A bronze plaque reads: *"October 24, 1959. We stayed."*

### 1960s: The Kingdom Builds
- **1960:** Donohue Manor is completed. Victor and Joan move in with newborn Jasmine.
- **Saturday, April 15, 1961: The Belmont Crest Country Club opens.** It is the first Black-owned private country club in the South with a championship course.
- **1962:** Belmont Crest Academy opens.
- **1963:** The **Magnolia Terrace Charter** passes. Simone insists that 40 lots be permanently reserved and sold at cost to Black teachers, doctors, dentists, nurses, and ministers: *"A kingdom with no teachers is just a fortress."* That's why the Bakers (a dentist and a principal) live there.
- **Throughout the Civil Rights Movement,** Belmont Crest quietly shelters movement leaders, lawyers, and entertainers in Atlanta, behind gates the Klan can't get through.

### 1970s–1980s: "Black Royalty"
- **1971:** *Ebony* runs a 12-page spread, **"Inside Belmont Crest: America's Black Royalty,"** which gives the family its lasting nickname.
- **1974–1988:** Kingsridge and Riverbend fill with Black athletes, entertainers, judges, and executives. A home in Belmont Crest becomes the ultimate status symbol in Black America.
- **1985:** *Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous* is denied entry at the Main Gate. Robin Leach reports from the sidewalk.

### 1990s–2004: The Modern Era
- **1992:** Belmont Crest Academy's championship debate team, including 10-year-old Marc-Anthony Bullock, wins the national title.
- **1996:** During the Olympics, Belmont Crest hosts a private gala for 40 heads of state.
- **1999:** Alexander dies at Donohue Manor.
- **2002:** Community security is contracted to **Blackwater Security International**, the new company of 20-year-old Marc-Anthony Bullock. Crime inside the gates drops from "almost none" to **exactly none.**
- **2003:** The Bullock Pavilion is built at 25 Sovereign Way.
- **2004:** Elxa Bullock marries Jeremy Jackson at the Chapel on **October 16**.

## Life Behind the Gates: Tone Notes for Writers
- Belmont Crest is paradise with sharp edges: manicured, silent, and watching.
- Security logs every entry. **BSI controls the security logs.** That matters.
- Staff see everything. The housekeepers' and drivers' network is the fastest gossip system in Atlanta.
- The Smilleys' estate, **Ravenhurst**, sits **just outside** the gates, across the Nancy Creek ravine off West Paces Ferry Road, bordering Belmont Crest's eastern wall (see Document 05). The two families can see each other's lights at night.
