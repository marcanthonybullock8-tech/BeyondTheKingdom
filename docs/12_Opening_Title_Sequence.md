# BEYOND THE KINGDOM — Opening Title Sequence

**Document 12 · Locked Canon · Main Titles & Opening Credits (Season 1)**

---

## Overview

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Length | **About 70 seconds** for Episodes #1–#89. **About 75 seconds** from Episode #90, when Alvin's sequence is added. |
| Placement | After the Teaser (cold open), before Act One |
| Theme | **"Kingdom (Main Title Theme)."** Orchestral strings, a gospel choir humming the melody, a four-note piano motif (*the Crown motif*), and Atlanta 808 drums underneath. It's old money over a new beat. |
| Concept | **"The Hall of Portraits."** The camera glides through the portrait gallery of Donohue Manor, and each gilded frame comes to life. |
| Who's credited | **All adult Contract and Featured Contract Cast** |
| Not credited in opening | Child performers (Emma, Victoria, Lyric), Recurring, Supporting, Day Players, and Guest Stars. They appear in the **closing credits**, following real daytime practice. |
| Billing order | Standard group in **alphabetical order by performer surname** (the real daytime convention, which keeps egos level). Until casting, this document orders by character surname. The **five signature sequences** always come last, in rising prestige. |

## Structure of the Sequence

| Timecode | Segment | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:07** | **Prologue: "Black Water"** | Night. The **Chattahoochee River** runs black beneath the Belmont Crest bluffs. A gold crown shimmers in the water, and a hand's reflection slowly passes over it. The piano plays the Crown motif. The **Crown Gate** swings open, and the camera passes the **scorched pillar** and its plaque: *"October 24, 1959. We stayed."* |
| **0:07–0:42** | **The Hall of Portraits (Standard Group)** | Inside Donohue Manor's portrait gallery. One continuous dolly move down a corridor of gilded frames. As each frame fills the screen, the painting dissolves into live action (2–3 seconds per actor), then turns back into a painting as the camera moves on. |
| **0:42–1:05** | **The Signature Sequences** | Alvin (from #90), Jasmine, Natasha, Joan, and Victor, each in a unique, fully produced vignette. |
| **1:05–1:12** | **Main Title** | Crane up over Buckhead at night: **Donohue Tower** and **Smilley Tower** face each other across Peachtree Road. The two towers' lights reflect in the river. The gold logo **BEYOND THE KINGDOM** rises between them, crowned. The choir holds the final note. |

### The Portrait Frame Code (a visual language the fans will decode)
- **Gold frames:** the Donohue family and their blood (the Bullocks and Olsons)
- **Silver frames:** the Smilleys and their circle
- **Bronze frames:** everyone else: friends, law, media
- **Easter egg:** In **Marc-Anthony's** gold frame, the painting's reflection in the glass shows a **black onyx coin** for 6 frames (a quarter-second). Only the sharpest fans on the message boards will catch it.
- **Easter egg:** **Esther's** silver frame has a **gold mask** hanging on the painted wall behind her.

## THE SIGNATURE SEQUENCES (Unique, 5 seconds each)

These five characters don't appear in the Hall of Portraits. Each gets a **unique, fully produced vignette** with its own **variation of the theme**, and a **billing card** that stays on screen longer. They're billed in rising prestige, and Victor comes last.

### 1. ALVIN DONOHUE — "The Prince" *(added in Episode #90, Monday, May 9, 2005)*
- **Music variation:** The theme drops into a **Miami bass / crunk** remix, the only time the titles break genre.
- **Shots:**
  1. Helicopter blades spin over a sunset ocean.
  2. Smash cut to the neon **VELVET** sign flickering on.
  3. Alvin sprays champagne over a VIP crowd in slow motion.
  4. He pulls his sunglasses down, grins at camera, and tosses the bottle to someone off-screen.
- **Final image:** The bottle lands in the hand of an elegant man in shadow. *(Julian Cross. Fans won't notice until July.)*
- **Billing card:** ***with* [Performer] *as* ALVIN DONOHUE**, in neon-gold script that flickers like a club sign
- **Why it's unique:** Alvin is the only character whose sequence is *loud*. He crashes the elegance of the titles the same way he crashes the family.

### 2. CONGRESSWOMAN JASMINE DONOHUE OLSON — "The Stateswoman"
- **Music variation:** A brass fanfare (a "Hail to the Chief" feel) over the choir
- **Shots:**
  1. Dawn over the **U.S. Capitol dome**.
  2. Jasmine strides through the **Capitol Rotunda**, her heels echoing, with staffers hurrying behind her.
  3. At the House podium she turns to a wall of camera flashes.
  4. In reverse angle, the flashes become stars on an American flag, which dissolves into the Georgia state flag, and then into a Georgia Governor's Mansion gate that she stops in front of and studies.
- **Billing card:** **[Performer] *as* CONGRESSWOMAN JASMINE DONOHUE OLSON**, in marble-white lettering carved like a Capitol inscription
- **Why it's unique:** It's the only sequence set outside Atlanta. Her power is national, and the gate at the end foreshadows her run for governor.

### 3. NATASHA DONOHUE BULLOCK — "The Empress"
- **Music variation:** The full orchestra hits the Crown motif hard, with a snapping percussive beat.
- **Shots:**
  1. The doors of the **DGH boardroom on the 88th floor of Donohue Tower** swing open.
  2. Natasha walks the length of the boardroom table in a white power suit as 30 executives stand.
  3. She drops a ring of **island keys** on the table without stopping.
  4. She sits at the head of the table, the Chief Executive's chair, turns to the window, and the whole world skyline is reflected in the glass: Atlanta, London, Paris, Lagos, Tokyo.
  5. She **winks**.
- **Billing card:** ***and* [Performer] *as* NATASHA DONOHUE BULLOCK**, with the subtitle *Chief Executive Officer, Donohue Global Holdings* appearing in small caps for one beat
- **Why it's unique:** It's the only sequence where a character breaks the fourth wall. Natasha bends every rule, including this one.

### 4. THE HONORABLE JOAN DONOHUE — "The Judge"
- **Music variation:** Solo cello and a single gospel voice carry the Crown motif, the quietest sequence.
- **Shots:**
  1. A black-and-white, 1960s-style shot: young Joan on the steps of a Southern courthouse with a briefcase, facing a hostile crowd.
  2. The shot bleeds into color as a gavel falls in slow motion in a federal courtroom.
  3. In the present day, Joan sits in the Donohue Manor library among law books, removes her reading glasses, and looks up with a small, knowing smile.
  4. Behind her, the photograph of her 1979 swearing-in catches the light.
- **Billing card:** ***and* [Performer] *as* THE HONORABLE JOAN DONOHUE**, in gold with a small scale-of-justice emblem
- **Why it's unique:** It's the only sequence that spans 40 years, a lifetime of moral authority in five seconds.

### 5. VICTOR DONOHUE — "The King" *(always last)*
- **Music variation:** The entire theme builds to its full crescendo, with orchestra, choir, and drums together.
- **Shots:**
  1. **Dusk**, in Victor's office on the **88th floor of Donohue Tower**. Victor stands at the window with his back to camera and his hands folded behind him.
  2. The camera slowly circles him. On the wall behind him hangs the **portrait of Alexander Donohue**.
  3. He lifts a **black king** from the chess board on his desk and sets it down in the middle of the board with a soft *click*.
  4. As he turns to face the camera, **Smilley Tower's lights switch on across Peachtree** and reflect in his glasses.
  5. He doesn't smile.
- **Billing card:** ***and* [Performer] *as* VICTOR DONOHUE**, in engraved gold with the subtitle *Chairman, Donohue Global Holdings*. It holds for a full two seconds, the longest card in the sequence.
- **Why it's unique:** Victor is the last face before the title, because every other story in the show happens in his kingdom.

### Special Episode Variations
- **Episode #1 (Premiere):** An extended 90-second version, with an extra aerial shot of the new Smilley Tower's opening morning.
- **Episode #35 (the "New Year's, 2002" flashback special):** The titles play in **black and white**. Arianna's frame is the only one in color.
- **Episode #100 ("Auburn Crown"):** The titles end on the **Hotel Donohue roof in the rain** instead of the two towers.
- **Episode #168 (The Storm):** No music. The Crown motif plays on solo piano over a black screen, with the notice: *"Dedicated to the people of the Gulf Coast."*
- **Episode #180 (Season Finale):** The standard titles play, but the last portrait frame, **Esther's**, cracks.

## CLOSING CREDITS (for reference)
- **Format:** A split screen. The episode's final scene freezes on the left while the credits roll on the right over the black-water river.
- **Order:**
  1. **Children:** Emma Smilley, Victoria Smilley, Lyric Solace Baker
  2. **Recurring Cast**
  3. **Supporting**
  4. **Day Players**
  5. **"Special Guest Star"** cards, including Theodore, Reginald, and Connie Donohue in Episodes #176–#180
  6. Crew
- **Closing theme:** A 30-second piano-and-choir reprise of "Kingdom."
