## THE ATLANTA SENTINEL — Saturday, January 8, 2005 · Weekend Edition · 75¢

### FRONT PAGE

> **SECOND 'BLACKWATER' SLAYING; FBI JOINS PROBE**
> *Check-cashing owner found dead with onyx coin; Chief names husband to lead task force*

*By Marcus Webb, Staff Writer*

A Southside check-cashing store owner was found shot to death Friday morning, the second killing in five days in which the victim was reportedly found holding a black onyx coin associated with the Blackwater Syndicate.

Earl Tuggle, 51, owner of Big E's Check Cashing & Payday Loans on Metropolitan Parkway, was found by his niece shortly after 6 a.m. Police said he died of a single gunshot wound late Thursday night.

Neighbors said Tuggle had been paying "protection" to the Syndicate for years. "That coin sat on his counter like a sign," said one merchant. "Everybody knew nobody would touch his store." Asked why the Syndicate would kill a man it protected, the merchant shrugged. "They wouldn't. That's what's scaring people."

Special agents from the FBI's Atlanta Field Office were seen at the scene. A Bureau spokesman confirmed "an ongoing federal investigation into organized criminal activity in the Atlanta region" but declined to elaborate.

At a Friday news conference, Chief Elxa Jackson named Detective Jeremy Jackson, her husband, to command the department's Blackwater Task Force. Asked about nepotism, the Chief cited his clearance record and offered to "hire someone better" if one could be found, "and Detective Jackson can take out the trash for a week." *(Continued on A8)*

### LIVING · "BEHIND THE GATES" by Bernadette Sinclair

**The Friday Night Nobody Will Talk About.**

Darlings, I have been writing this column for thirty-one years, and I have never seen this.

At 7:00 PM Friday, **Silas Merriweather**, the West End's most distinguished undertaker, walked through the front door of **Dee's Kitchen** on Ralph David Abernathy Boulevard. Longtime West Enders tell me he had not set foot in that restaurant since **1975**.

The dining room went silent. The proprietress, the legendary **Dolores Whitfield**, looked up from the counter and said two words: **"Friday, Silas."**

He took off his hat. He went into the back room. He stayed an hour and fifteen minutes. When he came out, a diner tells me, he was "laughing and full of pie."

I don't know what was said in that room. Nobody will tell me, and I have asked *everyone*. But I know this city, and I know that when those two sit down at the same table, the Southside can breathe again.

**Also spotted:** A certain **silver Aston Martin**, parked across the boulevard the whole time, its young driver eating fried chicken out of foil. Atlanta's richest bachelor, darlings, doing a Friday-night drive-through at Dee's like a regular citizen. How *democratic*.
